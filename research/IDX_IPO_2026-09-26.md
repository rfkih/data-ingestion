# IDX menu 46 - IPO patterns - 2026-09-26 - 10 trials (983..992), cumulative N = 992

Script `research/idx_ipo.py` (pre-registration in its docstring, written before any return was computed). Results cache
`tmp/ipo_results.pkl`, log `tmp/ipo_run.log`. Study row `ipo_patterns` #283.

**Verdict: 0 / 10 arms pass. The IPO family is CLOSED as a long-only source of return.** What the data do say, loudly, is the
opposite sign: a young IPO bought in the secondary market underperforms matched seasoned names by ~15 % over the next six months
(I3c, t -4.0 clustered by listing month, negative in 6 of 6 listing-year cohorts, median -18 %). That is an AVOID fact, not a trade.

- All 317 listings have bars from the listing day (313 exactly on it); none of the 2020+ IPOs is delisted, so survivorship is not an issue
  here (suspended names stay in; exit days without trading slide to the next traded day).
- Board on the listing day: 197 Pengembangan, 75 Utama, 45 Akselerasi. Akselerasi names were kept (8-11 % of events per arm); dropping
  them is a neighbour and changes nothing.
- IPO price = `daily_summary.previous` on the listing day. First-day close vs IPO price: median +24.8 %, 59 % close >= +20 %, 14 % below.
  That first-day pop belongs to e-IPO allottees; the desk cannot buy at the IPO price at size (pooled e-IPO allocation), so every
  entry here is a secondary-market buy at the closing offer, and the first buyable session after any ARA streak.
- **Lock-up rule, verified:** it is **POJK 25/POJK.04/2017 art. 2** (not "25/2018"): any party that acquired equity *below the IPO
  price within 6 months before filing the registration statement* may not transfer it until **8 months after the registration statement
  becomes EFFECTIVE** (the clock is the OJK effective date, not the listing date; central/regional government and bank-restructuring
  holders exempt). Controllers typically also commit in the prospectus to keep control for 12 months from the effective date (e.g.
  RLCO, BEI letter S-13571/BEI.PP2/12-2025: holders 8 months, controller 12 months), and IDX's draft I-A amendment (Feb 2026) moves the
  controller lock-up into the listing rule, counted from listing. Effective -> listing is 8-10 calendar days in 2025 prospectuses
  (DGNS 30-06 -> 10-07, COIN 30-06 -> 09-07, EMAS 15-09 -> 23-09), so LE = listing + 8 months - 9 days. The desk has no lock-up
  disclosures in `idx.announcement` (0 hits for lock-up/penguncian; the 239 prospectus rows carry no extracted text), so LE is a
  rule-derived date; neighbours shift it by 0 / -21 days and to the 12-month controller date.

## Battery

- **Independent cohorts.** Waves are bunched: 2023 alone is 79 IPOs (25 %), 2021-23 is 60 %. Events cluster into 32-61 listing months per
  arm; the entry-month clustering gives the same t, so calendar overlap does not change the reading. There are really six listing-year
  cohorts, and every long arm is negative in at least 4 of them.
- **Placebo A (same names, 500 draws of random eligible dates):** every arm sits at the 17th-70th percentile except I4a (99th) and I3b
  (92nd). Reading: the post-IPO names underperform at *any* date in their first years; the specific timing rules (dip, stabilisation,
  lock-up) add nothing to that. I4a's 99th percentile only says "strong first month" is less bad than a random date on the same names
  (-6.5 % vs a -13 % median); it is still negative.
- **Placebo B (random liquid seasoned names on the same dates vs their own matches, 500 draws):** placebo medians -1 to -3 %; every arm
  sits at the 0-5th percentile (I3b 30th). The underperformance is an IPO property, not the calendar or the matching.
- **Neighbours (not counted):** I1 drop 30/40/60 %, H40/120, floor 5 bn, no Akselerasi: 10/10 negative (t -0.7..-5.2). I2 LE = listing+8m,
  -21 d, H10/20/40, controller 12 m, floor: 9/9 negative. I3 profit-only, H60/250, s = 40, floor 5 bn: 10/10 negative. I4 thresholds
  +30/+100 %, <= -10/+10 %, H20/120: 7/8 negative; the one positive is I4a at H20 (+3.0 %, t 1.1).
- **Costs x1.5** move each arm by 0.5-0.8 pp; irrelevant next to the sign.
- **AVOID (I2c) for existing books:** holding an IPO over the 20 sessions into LE costs -4.3 % gross vs matches, but after the round trip
  a holder would pay to step aside the saving is -3.1 %, t -1.4, 2/6 cohorts positive, placebo A 17th pct (bar 5th) -> not a filter by the
  rule. The 12-month controller date is sharper (-6.7 %, t -2.9, neighbour only, not counted).

## Combo effect

Nothing passed, so no 4th-sleeve test was run (the combo baseline 33.8 % / 1.83 / -17.9 % is untouched).

## Reading

1. **The market prices the IPO before the desk can act - again, and here the price is too high.** The first-day pop (+25 % median) goes
   to allottees; from the first buyable session a secondary buyer loses to matched seasoned names in every cohort and at every horizon
   tried (60, 120, 250 sessions). This is the classic long-run IPO underperformance, and on IDX it is large (-15 %/6 months).
2. **No timing rule inside the IPO life rescues it.** Buying the 35-60 % slide after stabilisation, buying after the lock-up, buying
   quality issuers, buying first-month winners or broke issues: every one is negative; placebo A shows they are no better than a random
   date on the same names.
3. **Quality (I3) is the only arm that is not significantly negative**, and its only positive cohort is 2023 (+20 %, 24 events); 5 of 6
   cohorts lose. Do not reopen it with a finer quality screen: that would be accuracy-hunting on 96 events.
4. **The lasting use is a negative screen, and it is not tested here.** The ML sleeve is board-agnostic (#192) and can buy young IPOs;
   whether "no names within ~250 sessions of listing" helps the ML/trend books is a construction question for a future menu (it would
   cost trials; the prior from this study is favourable but the books' own entry rules may already avoid these names).
5. Menu 46 CLOSED. Trials used 10 (983..992), cumulative N = 992.
