# IDX — Three momentum names from 2008 (2026-09-14)

**Question.** The three-name momentum cut of the value list was the highest number in the catalog (29.5 % a year
2021–2026, IDX_TOP3_UPSIDE). The operator asked for it from 2008.

**Why not on IDX data.** The desk's bars *are* IDX data, but the exchange's own API serves nothing before 2020-01-02
(probed 2026-09-14: `GetStockSummary` answers 200 with zero rows for every date in 2008–2019, 671 rows from 2020-01-02)
and financial reports from FY2019 (`GetFinancialReport` returns one result for 2019, none for 2018 and earlier). So a
value list cannot be rebuilt before 2020, and before 2020 the only prices are the Yahoo cache: 450 names that still
exist today. Survivorship flatters a concentrated momentum rule most of all, because the names that went to zero after
their run are not in the file. The comparison below is on the same basket for every arm, so it is fair even where the
level is not; the level is generous to momentum.

**Verdict.** Over eighteen years the three momentum leaders of the liquid basket made 2.5 % a year on average across
four calendars with a 94 % worst drawdown, against 10.9 % and 54 % for the basket itself. Without each calendar's single
best name the average is −3.4 %. The crash filter does not save it (4.1 %, 87 %). Ten and twenty momentum names are also
below the basket. The three "most room to recover" names lose money outright (−3.6 % a year, 96 % drawdown). The 2021–2026
result was the exception, not the rule. `strict_top3_mom` stays in the catalog as *tested*, with this note attached.

## Pre-registered menu (5 trials; cumulative 151)

Basket: at each rebalance the 100 most traded names with a year of history (Yahoo cache). Annual rebalance, four
calendars (May, Feb, Aug, Nov), reset mode (equal weight), 25 bps + half-tick, price returns, 2008 to 2026-09. Script
`research/idx_momentum3_2008.py`; outputs `research-scratch/idx-screen/momentum3_2008_out.txt`, `momentum3_2008_results.json`.

Reading rule, declared before the run: mom3 is robust across regimes only if it beats the basket on CAGR in three of
four calendars, its 2008 and 2020 episode drawdowns are not more than 10 points deeper than the basket's, and the version
without its best name still beats the basket in three of four.

## Reading

1. **Momentum concentration is a crash amplifier.** The three leaders lose two thirds in 2008, two thirds in 2020 and
   three quarters in 2022, each time more than the basket, because the leaders going into a crash are the most extended
   names. The value list's leaders in 2021–2026 (PTRO, ENRG, TAPG, DSNG) happened in a window with no lasting crash.
2. **Diversifying the momentum cut helps but never catches the basket.** Ten and twenty names cut the drawdown from
   94 % to 65–84 % and lift the return to 7–9 %, still below plain equal weight. On this market, over this window, the
   momentum premium is not there after the crashes are counted.
3. **The crash filter removes 2008 and 2025 but not 2022**, when the three leaders (commodity names) fell 69 % while the
   index stayed above its average. A per-name concentration cannot be protected by an index rule.
4. **The November calendar is the one that looks good** (15–18 %), and it is one name (PANI's 2024–2025 run). The same
   pattern as every concentrated result in this project: the calendar that wins is the calendar that caught one stock.
5. **What this does not say.** Nothing here is about the value list, which cannot be tested before 2020. The
   eighteen-year evidence on price rules is: broad beats narrow, and momentum on top of a liquid basket does not pay.

## What changed

- `strict_top3_mom` keeps status *tested*; its catalog note carries the 2008 result. Trials: 151. No book changes.
- A real extension of the value backtest is available and cheap: IDX serves FY2019 audited workbooks, so downloading and
  parsing them would let the strict list start at the May 2020 rebalance (one more year, containing the post-crash
  rebound). Everything earlier needs a paid point-in-time fundamentals source.
