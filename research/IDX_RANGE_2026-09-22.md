## Reading (written after the run)

0 of 12 rule arms and 0 of 3 models. Every range-trading arm loses after costs, in every liquidity tier:

1. **The channel breaks more often than it holds.** Of 699 floor touches in liquid sideways names (1,350 in the thin tier), only 31-33 % reached
   the channel mid before the 5 % stop or the 60-day clock; the average trade is −0.9 to −1.1 % net (hit 43-47 %), and the costs (about 0.7 % a
   round trip in LIQ) explain less than half of it — the price path itself is negative. A name that has spent 120 days going nowhere in this
   market is, on average, resting before it goes down, not before it bounces; the same base rate the sleeper menus found (small caps −20 %/yr).
2. **Random entries in the same universe lose even more** (Sharpe −2 to −3 against −0.4 to −1.3 for the rules): the floor touch is a slightly
   better moment than a random day, but "slightly better than a losing universe" is still a loss.
3. **The operator's examples confirm it rather than contradict it.** SPTO: sideways 56 % of days, 15 touches, +1.8 % per trade at 47 % hit — the
   one positive, but it trades Rp 0.3 bn a day, below even the thin tier's floor, and its 2020 range was 116 %. MTEL: 9 touches, −2.3 %. PNIN:
   15 touches, −3.3 %, hit 13 %, range 37-219 % a year — a falling name that looks sideways in a long chart.
4. **The models see nothing.** LightGBM over 21 features: rank correlation with the outcome −0.09…+0.04, positive in 2 of 5 years, the filtered
   book still negative (−3.2 %/yr LIQ, −7.4 % THIN). The GRU on 60-day sequences: IC ≈ 0 in every fold. There is no structure in a floor touch
   that says whether it will bounce; the outcome is decided by what the market does next, which the touch does not know.

Closed. Range trading in IDX sideways names joins swing, support/resistance and sleepers: the spread is not the reason, the drift is.
