## Verdict (menu ML-3, study stored)

0 of 3 — and the reason is instructive. The model predicts ARA remarkably well: AUC 0.86–0.92 out of sample, precision@5 for
"ARA tomorrow" 6.5–18 % against a base rate of 0.3–0.9 % (lift 17–27×), twice the naive "up big on volume" rule. Yet buying its
top five every day loses 1.8 % per trade. The diagnostic on the picks (2024–2026):

| year | picks with an offer at the close | ARA hit among those | close→close of those | next open → next close |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 69 % | 2.6 % | −1.0 % | −1.4 % |
| 2025 | 41 % | 5.5 % | −0.4 % | −2.1 % |
| 2026 | 50 % | 5.1 % | −1.0 % | −1.7 % |

Half the model's top picks are names ALREADY locked at ARA today (the easiest prediction: a chain continues — 8 % of gain on
`days_since_ara`, 17 % on `ret1`). Those cannot be bought. Among the picks that can be bought, precision drops to 2.6–5.5 %
— under the 11 % break-even — and the next day is negative on average. Spread is not the problem (offer premium ~0.2 %, bid
discount ~0.3 %); the price path is. The model rediscovers menu 16 from the other side: what is predictable about ARA is
the part nobody can trade.

Not a model-class question: the label separates cleanly (AUC 0.9) and any classifier will find the same structure. A tradeable
version would need the model to score names BEFORE the run-up starts — from intraday data, once the feed has history.
